How it works

It reads the Bulletin so you don't

Every published program, every requirement, both current catalog years. Pick the one you entered under and your plan is held to those rules, not next year's.

It knows what unlocks what

Prerequisites are enforced, not suggested. Move a course earlier than something it depends on and the line between them turns red before you commit to it.

It finds the courses you're allowed to take

Click an empty slot and see real options you qualify for, with the ones that also close a General Education requirement marked.

Being straight with you

What it can't do.

It can't see when a class is offered

Penn State doesn't publish that in a form anyone can read automatically. Check LionPATH before you register.

It isn't your adviser

Some requirements are decided by a person, not a list. Where that's true, the planner says so instead of inventing an answer.

Some requirements it can't read

%COVERAGE_PERCENT%% of requirements across %COVERAGE_PROGRAMS% programs resolve to real, named courses. The rest are flagged for you to check rather than quietly filled in with a guess.

Measured against the %COVERAGE_EDITION% bulletin, not estimated. %COVERAGE_NAMED% requirements name their courses outright, and %COVERAGE_PATTERNED% give you a subject and a level to choose within. %COVERAGE_UNREADABLE% we could not read at all, and those are flagged for you rather than guessed at. %COVERAGE_GENED% are General Education buckets, filled from the designations a course carries.

Left out of that percentage altogether: the %COVERAGE_ADVISER% requirements where the Bulletin publishes no list and tells you to ask your adviser. Those are not failures to read something — there is nothing there to read — so the planner shows you the requirement's own wording instead. Counting them as misses would make the number look worse than the work is; counting them as hits would make it look better.
