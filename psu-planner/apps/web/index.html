LionsDegree reads Penn State's Bulletin, works out what unlocks what, and builds a four-year plan that actually holds. Drag a course and it tells you what breaks.

Free. No account. Your plan stays in your browser.

Pick your programs

Majors, minors and certificates, read from the Penn State bulletin itself: 759 programs across every campus. Add a second major and the plan folds it in, double-counting what genuinely double-counts.

Move a course, see what breaks

Drag CMPSC 465 a term earlier and the line to CMPSC 360 turns red, because you cannot take it yet. Prerequisites are read from the catalog text and evaluated against your plan, not assumed from the suggested sequence, which is itself sometimes wrong.

Bring your own AI

There is no chatbot to pay for here. Export your plan, point your own assistant at it, and ask it anything. It reads the plan and the catalog locally, on your machine. 

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "lionsdegree": {
      "command": "npx",
      "args": ["-y", "lionsdegree-mcp", "--plan", "/path/to/your-plan.json"]
    }
  }
}

Claude Desktop reads claude_desktop_config.json; Claude Code reads .mcp.json. Export your plan first with Menu → Save plan to a file. It needs no account, no key and no network.

How much of the bulletin it can actually read

%COVERAGE_PERCENT%% of requirements resolve to named courses

%COVERAGE_RESOLVED% of %COVERAGE_TOTAL% requirements across %COVERAGE_PROGRAMS% programs in the %COVERAGE_EDITION% bulletin. The rest are rows the parser could not read, and they are counted as failures here rather than quietly dropped.

A further %COVERAGE_ADVISER% requirements are left out of that fraction entirely, because the bulletin publishes no list for them: it says to decide with your adviser. They are not failures and they are not successes, so counting them either way would make this number mean less. The planner shows them with the bulletin's own wording rather than an empty list.

What it can't do

  • It does not know which terms a course actually runs. Check LionPATH before you rely on a plan.
  • Some requirements name no course list at all, because the bulletin says to ask your adviser. Those say so rather than guessing.
  • Where the catalog could not be read cleanly, it says it could not be read. It will not invent a course list to fill a gap.
  • It is not an audit. LionPATH and your adviser decide what counts.
