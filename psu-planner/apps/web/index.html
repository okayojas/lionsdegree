If a requirement cannot be verified, it says so instead of guessing.

%COVERAGE_PERCENT%% of requirements across %COVERAGE_PROGRAMS% programs resolve to real, named courses.

Measured against the %COVERAGE_EDITION% bulletin, not estimated. %COVERAGE_NAMED% requirements name their courses outright, and %COVERAGE_PATTERNED% give you a subject and a level to choose within. %COVERAGE_UNREADABLE% we could not read at all, and those are flagged for you rather than guessed at. %COVERAGE_GENED% are General Education buckets, filled from the designations a course carries.

Left out of that percentage altogether: the %COVERAGE_ADVISER% requirements where the Bulletin publishes no list and tells you to ask your adviser. Those are not failures to read something — there is nothing there to read — so the planner shows you the requirement's own wording instead.

About

Built by a student who got tired of the spreadsheet.

Planning a Penn State degree means holding four documents open at once: the Bulletin for what your major requires, a page per course for what it needs first, the General Education framework, and LionPATH for what you have already done. Nothing joins them up. So everyone builds the same spreadsheet, and everyone's is slightly wrong.

It is one person's side project, not a company — started in September 2026 and open source under Apache 2.0, so you can read exactly how every number on this page was produced.

The rule it is built on: never guess. Course data comes from a deterministic parse of the Bulletin's own markup, no language model ever writes into the catalog, and anything the parser cannot read is shown to you as unreadable rather than filled in with something plausible.
