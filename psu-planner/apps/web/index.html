Some requirements it can't read

%COVERAGE_PERCENT%% of requirements across %COVERAGE_PROGRAMS% programs resolve to real, named courses.

If a requirement can't be verified, it says so instead of guessing.

Measured against the %COVERAGE_EDITION% bulletin, not estimated. %COVERAGE_NAMED% requirements name their courses outright, and %COVERAGE_PATTERNED% give you a subject and a level to choose within. %COVERAGE_UNREADABLE% we could not read at all, and those are flagged for you rather than guessed at. %COVERAGE_GENED% are General Education buckets, filled from the designations a course carries.

Left out of that percentage altogether: the %COVERAGE_ADVISER% requirements where the Bulletin publishes no list and tells you to ask your adviser. Those are not failures to read something — there is nothing there to read — so the planner shows you the requirement's own wording instead. Counting them as misses would make the number look worse than the work is; counting them as hits would make it look better.

About

Built by a student who got tired of the spreadsheet.

Planning a Penn State degree means holding four documents open at once: the Bulletin for what your major requires, a separate page per course for what it needs first, the General Education framework, and LionPATH for what you've already done. Nothing joins them up. So everyone builds the same spreadsheet, and everyone's is slightly wrong.

LionsDegree is that spreadsheet, done properly and done once.

It's one person's side project, not a company — started in September 2026 and open source under Apache 2.0, so you can read exactly how every number on this page was produced.

The rule it's built on: never guess. Course data comes from a deterministic parse of the Bulletin's own markup, no language model ever writes into the catalog, and anything the parser can't read is shown to you as unreadable rather than filled in with something plausible.
